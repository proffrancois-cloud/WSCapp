# Product

## Register

product

## Users

WSCapp primarily serves World Scholar's Cup students who prepare alone or with a team, often on phones, tablets, school laptops, projectors, and uneven school Wi-Fi. Coaches, teachers, club leaders, and parents are secondary trust and facilitation audiences. Students need to understand the current theme, practise Challenge-style questions, prepare debates, review weak areas, and run collaborative study games without a paywall.

## Product Purpose

WSCapp is a free, unofficial preparation campus for the World Scholar's Cup. It brings the seasonal theme, guided study routes, raw source material, Alpacards, videos, quizzes, Debate Lab, and team games into one coherent route toward competition readiness. Success means that a scholar can quickly choose a useful activity, make visible learning progress, and study or play with a team while coaches and parents can trust the experience, privacy posture, and source transparency.

## Brand Personality

Playful, scholarly, and trustworthy. The route-to-Yale metaphor, alpaca character, tactile illustrated surfaces, and team-game language should make demanding study feel inviting and memorable. The voice should remain clear and age-respectful: charming without becoming childish, confident without implying official WSC endorsement, and energetic without creating pressure.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards that flatten the route metaphor into interchangeable cards and metrics.
- Childish edutainment, novelty controls, or decorative motion that gets between a scholar and the next study action.
- Dense, unlabeled game hubs where unavailable modes, student tasks, and coach controls look equally actionable.
- Student paywalls, paid performance boosts, targeted advertising to minors, public leaderboards without consent, or dark-pattern streak pressure.
- Interfaces that hide the app's unofficial status, sources, privacy expectations, or external-community handoffs.

## Design Principles

1. Make the next useful study action obvious within the first viewport.
2. Let the journey metaphor clarify progress and choices, never obscure them.
3. Reward learning depth as much as play, with useful feedback and clear next steps.
4. Earn adult trust while preserving student delight through privacy, source transparency, and honest status labels.
5. Work reliably across phones, school laptops, classroom projectors, keyboards, touch, and constrained networks.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA across the core journeys. Maintain keyboard access, visible focus, semantic and properly named dialogs, focus trapping and restoration, sufficient text and control contrast, 44 by 44 CSS-pixel touch targets where practical, text zoom resilience, reduced-motion alternatives, and layouts that remain usable across mobile landscape, tablet, desktop, and projector contexts. Avoid relying on color, hover, audio, or fine pointer control alone to communicate state.
